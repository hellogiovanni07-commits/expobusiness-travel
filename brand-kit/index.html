III

The name

One word, two capitals. The parent company is always named in full and always linked.

The rule

First mentionExpoBusiness Travel Services
After thatExpoBusiness: one word, capital E, capital B
The parentImmaculate Connections, linked to immaculateconnectionsph.com wherever it appears
Relationship line“The business-travel arm of Immaculate Connections” or “Powered by Immaculate Connections”

Why it matters

The two-colour split, navy “Expo” and orange “Business”, belongs to the logo only. In running text the name is set in one colour, the colour of the sentence around it.

Clients book us because a known Cebu company stands behind the trip. Naming Immaculate Connections, and linking it, is how we show that on every page.

IV

Voice

Plain British English for people who sign off travel budgets.

  • Plain.Short sentences. The word an HR officer would use, not a brochure word.
  • Exact.Dates, headcounts, departure cities. Numbers over adjectives.
  • Accountable.We say what we do and who does it. One coordinator, named.
  • Honest.Expected is labelled expected. Nothing we cannot prove.

We say, we don’t say

  • The organiser has not published the 2027 calendar yet. We confirm dates before anything is booked.

    Packages from ₱29,999 all-in.

  • Group visa filing, rooming lists and coaches, run by one coordinator.

House rules

  • British spelling.Organise, programme, itemised, centre, travellers. Dates as 15 April 2027.
  • No fixed prices.Every trip is quoted from the brief. No “from ₱”, no package prices, no “free”.
  • No unproven badges.DOT, IATA and PhilGEPS appear only once a real number is on file. Until then, nothing.
  • No invented claims.No client names or logos without written permission, no made-up numbers, no testimonials we cannot show.
  • China is a promotion, not the brand.Canton Fair and the China expos live in the home-page hero. Everywhere else we are corporate travel for Philippine companies.
  • Confirmed or expected.Every expo date says which it is. “Expected” until the organiser publishes.

    X

    Photography

    Places where business happens: halls, cabins, stages, meeting rooms. Always under a navy veil, so the type reads first.

    The four hero grounds

    • The veil.Navy left to right, 94% → 82% → 60%, plus a 90% fade up from the bottom. Copy sits on the dark side.
    • Real over stock.The current photos are Unsplash (free licence). Replace them with the company’s own trip photos as they come in.
    • No fake proof.Never a stock photo presented as our client, our team or our trip. No logos of companies we have not worked with.
    • Context, not clichés.Halls, cabins, stages, factories, hotel lobbies, passports. No globes, no suitcases on beaches, no handshake close-ups as heroes.

    Easing

    • Transform and opacity only.Nothing animates layout. One scroll handler, throttled to the frame.
    • Loops pause.Off-screen, in a hidden tab, or on hover. The hero and marquee stop when you cannot see them.
    • Reduced motion is respected.With prefers-reduced-motion everything shows its final state at once.
    • Once, not every time.Reveals and charts play the first time they are seen. Hover is the only replay.
